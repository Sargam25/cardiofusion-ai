While multi-modal diagnostic data (electrocardiograms, clinical demographics, laboratory biomarkers) are routinely acquired in clinical workflows, real-world deployment faces two critical bottlenecks: modality missingness (e.g., unavailable labs at point-of-care) and lack of transparent explainability.\n- **Objectives**: Develop and validate a modular, missing-modality-aware multimodal framework combining 12-lead ECG signals with clinical and laboratory predictors without fabricating cross-dataset patient linkages.\n- **Methods**: We establish: (1) a 1D residual CNN ECG feature extractor trained on PTB-XL (21,799 12-lead records) for 5-class superdiagnostic classification; (2) an isotonic-calibrated XGBoost classifier with SHAP explainability on clinical and laboratory records (UCI Heart Disease benchmark); and (3) a unified missing-modality-aware fusion architecture with modality dropout and explicit availability masks. Missing-modality simulation demonstrates graceful degradation under partial modality dropout compared to naive imputation.\n- **Conclusions & Clinical Impact**: Transparent handling of missing modalities combined with patient-level explainability provides a viable architecture for multimodal clinical decision support, while underscoring that retrospective benchmarks cannot substitute for prospective clinical validation.\n\n---\n\n### 1.
